Teaching Philosophy — In Practice

Translation and interpreting aren't mastered. They're earned.

One messy, real-world decision at a time. Here's how that belief shows up in five ways, in every class.

👇 Tap each one to open — you can open as many as you like at once

I keep it student-centred, so everyone feels safe enough to try, stumble, and try again. I listen first, support without judgment, and build a room where getting it wrong out loud is how people actually learn.

I keep it clear and structured, breaking down complex ideas with scaffolding instead of jargon. Nothing gets left as "figure it out yourself", every hard concept gets a way in.

I keep it practical and engaging, mixing real tools and live scenarios instead of static textbook examples. If it doesn't feel like real work, it doesn't stick.

I stay real-world focused, pulling straight from my own industry work in translation and interpreting. What I bring to class isn't theory I read about, it's work I did last week.

Ultimately, I'm building students who are industry ready, not just academically ready. The proof isn't a grade sheet, it's watching them go from "I hope this is right" to confidently defending their choices.
